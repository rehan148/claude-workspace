GLP-1 protocols are a series of scheduled step-ups. Getting them right is mostly a tracking problem: which week you are in, what the next step is and how you have tolerated the current one.

## Semaglutide (Ozempic, Wegovy, compounded)

| Weeks | Weekly dose |
|---|---|
| 1–4 | 0.25 mg |
| 5–8 | 0.5 mg |
| 9–12 | 1.0 mg |
| 13–16 | 1.7 mg |
| 17+ | 2.4 mg |

## Tirzepatide (Mounjaro, Zepbound, compounded)

| Weeks | Weekly dose |
|---|---|
| 1–4 | 2.5 mg |
| 5–8 | 5 mg |
| 9–12 | 7.5 mg |
| 13–16 | 10 mg |
| 17–20 | 12.5 mg |
| 21+ | 15 mg |

## Retatrutide (research protocols)

Protocols reported in research communities typically start at 0.5–1 mg weekly and step up slowly toward 4–12 mg. Because it is still investigational, log everything and step up conservatively.

## Converting compounded vials to units

Compounded GLP-1s come as vials in mg with bacteriostatic water added. Divide vial mg by water mL for concentration, then divide dose by concentration for mL; multiply by 100 for U-100 units. The [semaglutide calculator](/peptide-calculator/semaglutide) and [tirzepatide calculator](/peptide-calculator/tirzepatide) have presets for common vial sizes.

## What to track each week

- Dose in mg (not units) and the vial it came from
- Injection site (alternate abdomen and thigh, and sides)
- Weight and waist on the same day each week
- Side effects in the 72 hours after the dose
- Food noise, appetite and energy check-ins

## Deciding when to step up

Step up only after four weeks at the current dose **and** when side effects have settled. If the current dose is producing results, holding it is a valid choice. A tracker that shows the level curve and the side-effect log side by side makes this an evidence-based decision rather than a guess.

## Missed doses

If you are within a few days of the scheduled dose, take it and continue the schedule. If more than a week has passed, resume at the same dose but expect side effects to return briefly. If several weeks have passed, restart the titration from a lower step.
