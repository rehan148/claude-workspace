Reconstitution means dissolving a lyophilised (freeze-dried) peptide powder in sterile bacteriostatic water so it can be drawn into a syringe. The chemistry is simple; the errors come from the maths. This guide walks through both, and the [free reconstitution calculator](/peptide-calculator) does the arithmetic for you.

## What you need

- The peptide vial (powder), typically 2 mg, 5 mg or 10 mg
- Bacteriostatic water (0.9% benzyl alcohol)
- A mixing syringe (3 mL) and insulin syringes (U-100, 0.5 mL or 1 mL)
- Alcohol swabs

## Step 1: Choose the water volume

The amount of water sets the concentration and therefore how many units each dose will be. Pick a volume that makes your dose a round number.

| Vial | Water | Concentration | 250 mcg dose | 500 mcg dose |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 mg | 1 mL | 5 mg/mL | 5 units | 10 units |
| 5 mg | 2 mL | 2.5 mg/mL | 10 units | 20 units |
| 5 mg | 2.5 mL | 2 mg/mL | 12.5 units | 25 units |
| 10 mg | 2 mL | 5 mg/mL | 5 units | 10 units |

Larger volumes make small doses easier to measure accurately. Very small volumes waste less water but push doses into the 2–5 unit range where syringe error is proportionally larger.

## Step 2: Do the maths

1. **Concentration** = vial mg ÷ water mL. Example: 5 mg ÷ 2 mL = 2.5 mg/mL.
2. **Dose volume** = dose mg ÷ concentration. Example: 0.25 mg ÷ 2.5 mg/mL = 0.1 mL.
3. **Units** = dose volume × 100. Example: 0.1 mL × 100 = 10 units.
4. **Doses per vial** = vial mcg ÷ dose mcg. Example: 5,000 ÷ 250 = 20 doses.

Write the concentration on the vial label with the date. Everything downstream depends on it.

## Step 3: Mix

1. Swab both vial tops.
2. Draw the chosen water volume into the mixing syringe.
3. Insert the needle into the peptide vial at an angle and let the water run **down the glass wall**, not onto the powder.
4. Do not shake. Roll gently or leave the vial for 10–20 minutes until fully clear.

## Step 4: Store and label

Refrigerate at 2–8 °C, protected from light. Label with compound, concentration and date. Most reconstituted peptides are used within about four weeks; discard anything cloudy or discoloured.

## Step 5: Log it

Enter the vial in your tracker with the concentration and date. From then on every logged dose should decrement the vial so you know when to reorder. In Pepti this happens automatically when you save a calculation to a protocol.

## Common mistakes

- **Logging units instead of mcg.** Units are volume. If you change water volume next vial, the same units deliver a different dose.
- **Forgetting the reconstitution date.** Old solution is the usual cause of “this vial felt weaker”.
- **Squirting water onto the powder.** It can foam and degrade some peptides.
- **Confusing mg and mcg.** 1 mg = 1,000 mcg. The calculator keeps them straight.
