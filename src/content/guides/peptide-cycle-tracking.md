A cycle is a protocol with a start date, an end date and, often, phases in between. Tracking a cycle well means knowing which phase you are in, how many doses remain and when to review.

## Common cycle structures

**On/off cycles.** Growth-hormone secretagogues such as ipamorelin and CJC-1295 are commonly run 8–12 weeks on, 4 weeks off. Nootropic peptides like Semax and Selank are run in shorter 2–4 week blocks.

**Loading then maintenance.** TB-500 and Melanotan II start with a higher-frequency loading phase, then drop to a lower maintenance frequency. The schedule change is where doses get missed or doubled.

**Short intense cycles.** Epitalon is run for 10–20 days once or twice a year. Multi-year history matters more than reminders.

**Continuous with titration.** GLP-1s are not cycled; they are stepped up on a schedule. See the [GLP-1 titration guide](/guides/glp-1-dose-schedule-and-titration).

## Tracking a stack

The classic recovery stack is BPC-157 (daily or twice daily) with TB-500 (twice weekly then weekly). The classic GH stack is CJC-1295 with ipamorelin, often in one syringe nightly. Track each compound as its own schedule, then log the combined injection with one tap. This keeps per-compound inventory accurate and lets the level curves stay separate.

## What to review at the end of a cycle

- Adherence: doses taken versus scheduled
- Outcome metrics you chose at the start: weight, measurements, sleep, recovery, labs
- Side effects by phase
- Cost per cycle from vial inventory

Write a two-line verdict and keep it with the cycle. The next time you consider the same stack, the log answers the question.
