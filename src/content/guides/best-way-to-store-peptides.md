Peptides are fragile molecules. Heat, light, agitation and time all degrade them, and a degraded vial is the most common explanation for a protocol that “stopped working”.

## Lyophilised powder

Store sealed vials in the refrigerator at 2–8 °C, or in the freezer for long-term storage. Keep them in the original box to block light. Allow a vial to reach room temperature before opening it to avoid condensation on the powder.

## Reconstituted solution

Refrigerate immediately at 2–8 °C. Do not freeze reconstituted peptides. Label each vial with the compound, concentration and date. Commonly cited shelf life with bacteriostatic water is about four weeks; discard anything cloudy, discoloured or with visible particles.

## Compound-specific notes

- **GHK-Cu:** light-sensitive; the blue solution fades as it degrades.
- **Glutathione and NAD+:** short stability once mixed; reconstitute smaller amounts.
- **GLP-1s (compounded):** typically stable for the 4–6 weeks it takes to use a vial when refrigerated.
- **TRT (oil-based):** room temperature, away from light.

## Travel

Use an insulated pouch with a cold pack for anything reconstituted. Powder can travel at room temperature for short trips. Carry a copy of your protocol; a tracker that exports a PDF makes this trivial.

## Track the dates

The simplest protection is a log that records the reconstitution date per vial and shows the age of the solution beside the doses remaining. Pepti flags vials that are approaching four weeks and lets you retire them with one tap.
