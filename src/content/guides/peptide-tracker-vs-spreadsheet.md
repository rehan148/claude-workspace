Every peptide user starts with notes or a spreadsheet. Some stay there; most move to an app after the first missed dose or the first vial that ran out unexpectedly. Here is a fair comparison.

## Notes app

Fast to start and always with you. Fails as soon as you need to answer “how many doses are left” or “what was I taking in March”. Best for a single as-needed compound like PT-141.

## Spreadsheet

Flexible and free. Use these columns:

`Date | Time | Compound | Dose (mcg) | Vial ID | Concentration (mg/mL) | Units | Site | Notes`

Add a second sheet for vials: `Vial ID | Compound | mg | Water (mL) | Concentration | Reconstituted on | Doses remaining`.

Strengths: complete control, exportable. Weaknesses: no reminders, manual unit maths, no rotation help, charts need formulas, and it is awkward on a phone at 6 am.

## Dedicated peptide tracker app

Purpose-built for the workflow: protocol schedules, reminders, a reconstitution calculator, a rotation map, inventory that decrements itself, half-life level curves and progress charts.

| Need | Notes | Spreadsheet | Tracker app |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reminders | No | No | Yes |
| Units per vial | No | Formula | Automatic |
| Rotation | No | Manual | Body map |
| Level curves | No | Complex | Automatic |
| Phase changes | No | Manual | Automatic |
| Export | Copy | Native | CSV/PDF |
| Cost | Free | Free | Free tier |

## Recommendation

Single compound, fixed schedule, no vial maths: a spreadsheet is fine. Anything else: use a tracker. Pepti’s free plan covers one full protocol with reminders, calculator, rotation and inventory, so the upgrade from spreadsheet to app costs nothing.
