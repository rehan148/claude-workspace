Half-life is the time for the amount of a compound in the body to fall by half. It determines how often you dose, how long it takes to reach a stable level and what a missed dose actually costs.

## Half-lives of common compounds

| Compound | Approximate half-life | Typical frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Semaglutide | ~7 days | Weekly |
| Tirzepatide | ~5 days | Weekly |
| Retatrutide | ~6 days | Weekly |
| Testosterone cypionate | ~8 days | 1–3× weekly |
| TB-500 | Days | 1–2× weekly |
| CJC-1295 DAC | ~6–8 days | Weekly |
| BPC-157 | ~4 hours | 1–2× daily |
| PT-141 | ~2.7 hours | As needed |
| Ipamorelin | ~2 hours | 1–3× daily |
| CJC-1295 no DAC | ~30 minutes | 1–3× daily |
| Tesamorelin | ~30 minutes | Daily |
| Sermorelin | ~10–20 minutes | Nightly |

Figures are commonly cited approximations; individual pharmacokinetics vary. See each [compound page](/track) for details.

## Steady state takes about five half-lives

Each dose adds to what remains from previous doses. After roughly five half-lives the amount added per dose equals the amount cleared, and levels plateau. This is why GLP-1 titration steps are four weeks apart: the previous dose has only just reached steady state.

Short half-life compounds never accumulate. A daily BPC-157 dose is essentially gone before the next one, so timing relative to training or meals is the lever, not accumulation.

## Reading a level curve

A level curve plots estimated concentration over time from your logged doses and the half-life. It shows:

- The five-week ramp on a weekly GLP-1
- The dip after a late or missed dose and how long it takes to recover
- Whether twice-daily dosing gives smoother coverage than once-daily for a short-acting compound

Pepti builds this curve automatically for every compound in your log, so “I feel worse this week” can be checked against “my level dipped after Tuesday’s late dose”.

## Using half-life to plan a protocol

- **Weekly compounds:** fix the day and time; a consistent weekly slot beats a precise hour.
- **Daily compounds:** fix the time relative to meals or sleep.
- **Tapering off:** long half-life compounds fade slowly on their own; short ones stop almost immediately.
