Most peptide protocols fail on logistics, not chemistry. Doses get skipped, vials run out mid-week, sites get overused and nobody can remember whether the good sleep started before or after the dose change. This guide sets out a tracking system that catches all of that, whether you use a notebook, a spreadsheet or a peptide tracker app.

## What to record for every dose

Record these five fields every time, without exception:

1. **Date and time.** Timing matters for short half-life compounds and for anything taken fasted or before bed.
2. **Compound and dose.** Log the delivered amount in mcg or mg, not the syringe units. Units depend on the concentration of that particular vial.
3. **Vial.** Which vial the dose came from, so you know remaining doses and the reconstitution date.
4. **Injection site.** Left or right, and the area. This is what makes rotation possible.
5. **Notes.** Anything unusual: a missed morning dose, a sore site, a new side effect.

Everything else is optional but valuable: weight, waist, sleep quality, energy, appetite, mood and any lab result.

## Structure the protocol before you start

A protocol is a schedule, not a list of compounds. Write down:

- **Frequency:** daily, twice daily, every other day, specific weekdays or weekly.
- **Dose per administration**, and whether it changes over time (titration).
- **Phases:** a loading phase and a maintenance phase, or an on-cycle and an off-cycle, each with a start date.
- **Duration:** when the cycle ends and when you will review it.

For a stack, write this out for each compound. Ipamorelin might be nightly for 12 weeks while the CJC-1295 DAC it is stacked with is weekly. Treat them as two schedules that happen to share an injection.

## Track vials, not just doses

A 5 mg vial reconstituted with 2 mL is 2.5 mg/mL. At 250 mcg per dose that is 20 doses. If you also log the reconstitution date you know two things at a glance: how many doses remain and how old the solution is. Reorder when you have a week of doses left, not when the vial is empty.

## Rotate injection sites systematically

Rotation prevents lumps, bruising and inconsistent absorption. Divide the abdomen into quadrants, add both thighs and both upper arms, and move through them in a fixed order. The [injection site rotation guide](/guides/injection-site-rotation) has a diagram and a simple sequence.

## Review weekly, decide monthly

Once a week, look at adherence (doses taken versus scheduled), remaining inventory and any side-effect notes. Once a month, compare the trend lines: weight, measurements, sleep, labs. Change one variable at a time so the log can tell you what caused what.

## Spreadsheet versus peptide tracker app

A spreadsheet is fine for a single compound on a fixed schedule. It breaks down when you need reminders, phase changes, units per vial, rotation and charts. A dedicated peptide tracker handles all of that:

| Task | Spreadsheet | Peptide tracker app |
|---|---|---|
| Reminders | No | Yes, per compound |
| Units per dose | Manual formula | Calculated per vial |
| Site rotation | Manual | Body map with next-site suggestion |
| Level curves | No | From half-life and your log |
| Inventory | Manual | Decrements on every log |
| Phase changes | Manual | Automatic on the phase date |
