Injecting the same spot repeatedly causes lumps, bruising, scar tissue and inconsistent absorption. A rotation system fixes all four problems and takes ten seconds per dose to follow.

## Approved subcutaneous zones

- **Abdomen:** four quadrants, at least 5 cm from the navel. Easiest to reach and most consistent absorption.
- **Thighs:** front and outer, left and right. Good for daily compounds when the abdomen is tender.
- **Upper arms:** back of the arm, left and right. Useful for weekly GLP-1 shots.
- **Love handles / flank:** left and right, an alternative for lean users.

Intramuscular injections (TRT, some TB-500 protocols) use the glutes, ventrogluteal area, deltoids and outer thighs, and rotate on the same principle.

## An eight-site rotation sequence

Number the zones and move through them in order:

1. Abdomen upper left
2. Abdomen upper right
3. Thigh left
4. Thigh right
5. Abdomen lower left
6. Abdomen lower right
7. Arm left
8. Arm right

For once-daily dosing this returns to any site every eight days. For twice-daily dosing, use the sequence for the morning dose and the reverse sequence for the evening dose so the two never land near each other.

## Weekly GLP-1 injections

Alternate abdomen and thigh, and alternate sides each week: abdomen left → thigh right → abdomen right → thigh left. Each site is used once a month.

## Signs a site needs rest

Skip any site that is bruised, hard, warm or itchy. Note it in your log; recurring reactions in one zone usually mean the needle is too shallow or the site is being reused too soon.
